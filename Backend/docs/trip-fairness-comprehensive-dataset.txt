# TripFairness AI: Comprehensive Group Travel Expense Settlement & Fairness Dataset

## 1. Domain Specification & Intelligence Framework
TripFairness AI is a specialized financial intelligence layer for group travel, vacation ledgers, shared bookings, and multi-party communal dining. It resolves complex social and mathematical frictions by enforcing:
1. Exact Net Balance Invariants: Sum of all net ledger balances across all participants must equal zero (sum(netBalance_i) == 0).
2. Upfront Fronting Equity via Gini Coefficient: Measuring the inequality of capital outlay among members.
3. Financial Stress Index (FSI): Early detection of fronting fatigue and credit concentration before trip completion.
4. Minimal Cash Flow Settlement: Eliminating redundant transactions using greedy bipartite graph reduction (Min-Transfers).
5. Ledger Integrity & Anomaly Detection: Flagging duplicate transactions, circular refunds, and disproportionate booking spikes.

## 2. Core Mathematical Formulations

### 2.1 Net Balance Equations
For traveler i in group P of size n:
- totalPaid(i) = sum of booking and expense payments contributed upfront by i
- totalShare(i) = sum of cost shares allocated to i across all items (equal or custom splits)
- netBalance(i) = totalPaid(i) - totalShare(i)
- When netBalance(i) > 0: Creditor. The group owes money to traveler i.
- When netBalance(i) < 0: Debtor. Traveler i owes money to the group.

### 2.2 Gini Coefficient for Fronting Inequality
G = [2 * sum(k * paid_sorted[k]) - (n + 1) * sum(paid_sorted)] / [n * sum(paid_sorted)]
Where paid_sorted is sorted in ascending order for k = 1 to n.
- G < 0.35: Healthy, egalitarian fronting distribution.
- 0.35 <= G <= 0.60: Moderate concentration. Advisory: Encourage secondary members to pay for upcoming dining/activities.
- G > 0.60: Critical concentration. 1 or 2 travelers are shouldering over 80% of upfront cash obligations.

### 2.3 Financial Stress Index (FSI)
A composite index scored from 0 to 100:
- Fronting Factor (0 to 40 pts): Top fronting ratio = max(paid(i)) / totalTripSpend. >50% gives 25 pts; >70% gives 40 pts.
- Debt Saturation (0 to 30 pts): Total unsettled debt / totalTripSpend. >40% gives 15 pts; >75% gives 30 pts.
- Anomaly Penalty (0 to 30 pts): Duplicate entries add 20 pts; extreme outlier expenses add 10 pts.
Risk Levels:
- 0 to 29: Low (Nominal group equity)
- 30 to 49: Moderate (Mild imbalance, normal progress)
- 50 to 69: High (Action needed: fronting load skewed)
- 70 to 100: Critical (Immediate settlement required to prevent discord)

### 2.4 Greedy Min-Cash-Flow Algorithm
1. Separate participants into Creditors (net > 0) and Debtors (net < 0).
2. Sort Creditors descending by credit; sort Debtors descending by absolute debt.
3. Match top debtor with top creditor, transferring min(|debt|, credit).
4. Update balances and repeat until all net balances are 0.
5. Invariant: Produces at most (n - 1) transactions for n travelers.

## 3. Training Scenarios and Real Ledger Case Studies

### Scenario 1: Goa Beach Villa Weekend (Balanced Splitting)
- Participants: Alice (id: u1), Bob (id: u2), Charlie (id: u3), Diana (id: u4)
- Expenses:
  - Villa Booking: Rs 24,000 paid by Alice, split equally among all 4 (Rs 6,000 each).
  - Seafood Dinner: Rs 8,000 paid by Bob, split equally among all 4 (Rs 2,000 each).
  - Scooter Rentals: Rs 4,000 paid by Charlie, split equally among all 4 (Rs 1,000 each).
  - Groceries & Drinks: Rs 4,000 paid by Diana, split equally among all 4 (Rs 1,000 each).
- Total Spend: Rs 40,000. Each member's share: Rs 10,000.
- Net Balances:
  - Alice: Paid 24,000, Share 10,000 -> Net +14,000 (Creditor)
  - Bob: Paid 8,000, Share 10,000 -> Net -2,000 (Debtor)
  - Charlie: Paid 4,000, Share 10,000 -> Net -6,000 (Debtor)
  - Diana: Paid 4,000, Share 10,000 -> Net -6,000 (Debtor)
- Settlement Plan:
  1. Charlie pays Alice Rs 6,000.
  2. Diana pays Alice Rs 6,000.
  3. Bob pays Alice Rs 2,000.
- Gini: 0.42. FSI: 38 (Moderate). Advice: Alice fronted 60% of trip costs; direct all remaining settlements to Alice.

## 4. Anomaly Detection and Ledger Integrity

### Anomaly Type A: Duplicate Payment Submissions
When two payment records with identical sender, receiver, and amount occur within short succession (e.g. UPI double-tap):
- Detection Rule: (fromMemberId == prior.fromMemberId && toMemberId == prior.toMemberId && amount == prior.amount && timeDelta < 24h)
- Resolution: Flag as critical anomaly. Halt automatic balance deduction until confirmed by payer and receiver.

### Anomaly Type B: Unusually Disproportionate Single Expense
- When an individual booking exceeds 40% of the entire trip budget, tag for explicit breakdown validation.
- Prevents accidental decimal errors (e.g. typing 50,000 instead of 5,000 for a local taxi).

## 5. Alternative Settlement Strategies
1. Min Transfers (Bipartite Optimization): Minimizes transaction count to (n - 1). Recommended default for friends and travel peers.
2. Organizer Hub: All debtors send funds to the trip leader/organizer; the organizer disburses to all creditors. Best for corporate or structured group tours.
3. Threshold Filtering: Any micro-debt below Rs 20 is absorbed or rounded down to eliminate annoying micro-transactions.
